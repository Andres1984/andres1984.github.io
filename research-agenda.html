Research Agenda

How are asset prices formed when information is incomplete, noisy, and shaped by investor behavior?

My research agenda develops this question through a narrative that connects asset valuation, behavioral finance, Bayesian statistics, and data science. Rather than studying prices as purely mechanical outcomes, I examine how they emerge from the interaction between information, expectations, market structure, and uncertainty.

This page is designed as a visual and conceptual story. Each section can be connected to animations that show how signals enter the market, how investors react, how submarkets emerge, and how prices are formed through the interaction between signal, bias, volatility, and noise.

The Story of Price Formation

01

Markets begin with information

Financial markets do not begin with prices. They begin with signals. In traditional settings, institutions such as central banks, regulators, and financial intermediaries provide structured information that helps investors interpret the economy and form expectations about the value of assets.

My research starts from this idea: price formation depends on how information is organized, interpreted, and transmitted across the market.

Animation placeholder: floating points or signals entering the market, initially dispersed, then forming structured paths or anchors.
02

Investors do not react in the same way

Once information enters the market, investors process it through expectations, beliefs, strategic behavior, and emotional responses. This is where behavioral finance becomes central. Herding, information cascades, and speculative reactions can amplify trends and reshape market dynamics.

Instead of assuming homogeneous agents, I study how heterogeneous reactions help create submarkets, local biases, and collective movements.

Animation placeholder: clusters forming from investor interactions, with some points changing color as agents imitate or respond to the same signal.
03

Prices emerge from signal, structure, and noise

Prices are not only reflections of fundamentals. They are also shaped by uncertainty, volatility, and the way investors coordinate their actions. In this sense, noise is not simply a disturbance; it is part of the market process itself.

A key objective of my work is to understand how market prices emerge from the interaction between meaningful signals, behavioral biases, and stochastic fluctuations.

Animation placeholder: red core for structural signal, orange ring for market price, gray or blue points for residual noise, and local submarket dynamics.
04

Uncertainty requires probabilistic models

Because markets are uncertain, the modeling framework must represent uncertainty explicitly. This is why Bayesian statistics plays a central role in my research. Bayesian methods allow the integration of prior knowledge, observed data, and latent market structures in a coherent way.

Through hierarchical models, stochastic volatility frameworks, and Bayesian learning, I study how risk, heterogeneity, and information interact across assets and over time.

Animation placeholder: dynamic updating of distributions, latent layers becoming visible, or points reorganizing as information is learned.
05

Data science helps reveal hidden structure

Data science complements financial theory by providing tools to detect nonlinear patterns, classify structures, and build predictive systems. In my research, machine learning is not used as a substitute for economic reasoning, but as a bridge between theory, evidence, and decision-making.

This allows me to connect pricing models with visual narratives that explain how markets learn, differentiate signal from noise, and generate observable price paths.

Animation placeholder: all points begin in one color, then the algorithm gradually separates structure, signal, and residual noise.

Methods and Foundations

Research Foundations

My work is grounded in asset valuation, behavioral finance, stochastic modeling, and Bayesian inference. Across these areas, I aim to build models that remain economically meaningful while being statistically rigorous.

Methodological Toolkit

Bayesian hierarchical models, stochastic volatility, risk-factor models, market microstructure, machine learning, data visualization, and computational methods for uncertainty quantification.
