Andrés Martínez, Ph.D.

Research Professor in Data Science | Department of Mathematics, Universidad Externado de Colombia

Markets do not begin with prices, they begin with information, expectations, and uncertainty. My research is driven by a central question: how are asset prices formed when information is incomplete, noisy, and shaped by investor behavior?

To answer this question, I work at the intersection of asset valuation, behavioral finance, Bayesian statistics, and data science, developing models that explain how signals, beliefs, and volatility interact to produce observed prices in both traditional and emerging markets.

Rather than treating noise as a disturbance, I study it as part of the pricing process itself. This perspective helps explain how markets organize information, how investors coordinate their decisions, and how prices emerge from the interaction between structure and uncertainty.

About

Andrés Martínez is a finance and economics scholar with advanced training in stochastic engineering and management research. He serves as a full-time research professor in the undergraduate Data Science program at the Department of Mathematics, Universidad Externado de Colombia.

He holds an M.Sc. in Stochastic Engineering from Munich University of Applied Sciences, Germany, and a doctorate in Management from Universidad de los Andes. His academic work bridges quantitative finance and data science, with a focus on asset valuation, Bayesian statistics, risk modeling, blockchain, financial derivatives, and computational approaches for decision-making under uncertainty.

In addition to his undergraduate teaching, he currently teaches Bayesian Statistics, Bayesian Statistics for Machine Learning, and Asset Pricing. He also coordinates executive education initiatives and works on building research narratives that combine rigorous modeling, behavioral interpretation, and visual storytelling.

Research

My research agenda develops statistical and economic tools to understand how prices are formed when markets are volatile, information is heterogeneous, and investor behavior matters.

Research question: How are asset prices formed when information is incomplete, noisy, and shaped by investor behavior? This question connects my work on asset valuation, market dynamics, Bayesian modeling, and behavioral finance.

Asset Valuation

I study price formation in traditional and emerging markets using stochastic volatility models, risk-factor approaches, and market microstructure perspectives. A central line of my research examines how volatility dynamics, exposure to risk, and uncertainty shape observed prices.

Behavioral Finance

I analyze how information cascades, herding behavior, speculative expectations, and sentiment affect trading decisions and price dynamics. This perspective is especially relevant in markets where institutional anchors are weak and beliefs play a stronger role.

Bayesian Statistics & Data Science

I use Bayesian hierarchical models, causal inference, stochastic processes, nonparametric methods, and machine learning tools to estimate risk, forecast returns, quantify uncertainty, and identify hidden structures in financial data.

Teaching

My teaching integrates theory, computation, and practical problem solving. I aim to help students build strong quantitative foundations while connecting models to real decision contexts.

I teach undergraduate and graduate courses in data science, finance, and applied statistics. My classes often combine mathematical reasoning, coding, and applied interpretation, especially in areas where uncertainty, risk, and prediction are central.

Teaching Philosophy

I believe that rigorous quantitative education should remain economically meaningful and computationally relevant. For that reason, I emphasize conceptual clarity, reproducible analysis, and the interpretation of models in real institutional and market settings.
